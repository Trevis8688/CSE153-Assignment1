Task 1 - Composer classification:
Each MIDI file is summarized with 94 features: pitch, duration, velocity, melodic interval, and inter-onset interval statistics, plus a pitch-class histogram, a register histogram, an interval histogram, a duration histogram, polyphony statistics, and tempo / time-signature / note-density information. A logistic regression, a random forest, and a gradient boosting classifier are trained on these features, and their predicted probabilities are averaged to pick the final composer.

Task 2 - Temporal order prediction:
Each segment is summarized (first and last note pitch and velocity, head and tail pitch means, pitch-class histogram, overall statistics). For a pair of segments, the features compare the segment-1-to-segment-2 transition against the segment-2-to-segment-1 transition (pitch jumps, head-versus-tail differences, velocity continuity, pitch-class similarity). Because swapping a pair flips its label, the training data is doubled with the swapped pairs, and the same logistic regression / random forest / gradient boosting ensemble is trained.

Task 3 - Music tagging:
Each 10-second clip is loaded as a mono waveform at 32 kHz and fed directly into a CNN14 backbone that was pretrained on AudioSet. The original 527-class classifier is discarded and the 2048-dimensional embedding produced by the backbone is passed through a small two-layer head (linear, ReLU, dropout, linear) that outputs the ten tag logits. The backbone and the head are fine-tuned end to end with binary cross-entropy on the logits, using a smaller learning rate (0.0001) for the pretrained backbone and a larger one (0.001) for the fresh head, the AdamW optimizer, and a cosine learning-rate schedule for 25 epochs. Each training batch is augmented with mixup at alpha 0.4, and a 90/10 train and validation split keeps the checkpoint with the best validation mean average precision.
